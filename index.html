Hello there! I am Yihao Zhou (周亦豪), a final year Astrophysics PhD student and FINESST Fellow at Carnegie Mellon University.

Research Interests

I work with Prof. Tiziana Di Matteo, using cosmological simulations to study how massive black holes (MBHs) form, grow, and merge. My goal is to turn these simulations into predictions and interpretations for the observations that are reshaping the field: the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), Pulsar Timing Arrays (PTAs), and the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA).

To do this, I build simulations across an enormous range of scales — from sub-parsec to Mpc, and from the first billion years to the present-day universe:

  • ASTRID — one of the largest hydrodynamical cosmological simulations evolved all the way to the present day.
  • AMBRA — an ASTRID-volume run with physically motivated seeding, tracing the first massive black holes.
  • MAGICS — high-resolution follow-ups that resolve MBH dynamics down to sub-parsec scales.
