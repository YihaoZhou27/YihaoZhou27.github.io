Selected projects and their key findings

2025

The ASTRID Simulation at z=0

ASTRID is one of the largest hydrodynamical cosmological simulations evolved to $z=0$. It hosts $2\times5500^3$ particles in a box size of 250 Mpc/h per side. ASTRID features a large population of massive black holes (MBHs), ranging from $10^4$ to $10^{11}$ M$_\odot$, and a black hole dynamical friction model that properly describes the MBH orbital evolution. This makes it a powerful tool to study the formation and evolution of MBHs in a cosmological context.

2024

MAGICS: Massive black hole Assembly in Galaxies Informed by Cosmological Simulations

To bridge the gap between cosmological simulations and small-scale MBH dynamics, we developed MAGICS. We reproduce merging systems in ASTRID with high-resolution idealized simulations, and implement better gravity solver: regularized integrator KETJU and $N$-body code TAICHI. This allows us to study the MBH dynamics on sub-parsec scales, which gives a better description of the MBH merger process.

2026

AMBRA: ASTRID with MBH seeding from BRAMA

AMBRA combines the large cosmological volume and statistical power of ASTRID with the physically motivated gas-based black hole seeding models from BRAHMA. Motivated by JWST's discoveries of massive black holes (BHs) at $z\gtrsim 9$, AMBRA adopts a lenient heavy-seed prescription from the BRAHMA suite, allowing for the formation of $4\times 10^{4-5}\ M_{\odot}$ seeds in halos with star-forming, metal-poor gas. The seeding model is motivated by scenarios in which heavy seeds form through stellar collisions in star clusters or from the rapid growth of Population III remnants. The improved seeding model enables AMBRA to form BH seeds much earlier and more efficiently compared to ASTRID.

2024

PTA Continuous Wave Sources

We uses the MBH population in ASTRID to predict the continuous wave (CW) sources for the PTA experiments. We found all the high-detectability CW sources are from the central galaxies of the massive galaxy clusters.

2023

Predicting LISA Detection Rates

Based on ASTRID, we predicted the detection rates of the LISA experiment. We found that considering the evolution of MBH orbital eccentricity, the black hole mass range explored by LISA is extended to high mass end: $\approx 10^9$ M$_\odot$.
